# Swell Paper and Embossed Tactile Graphics

Words alone cannot replace bar charts, circuit schematics, transit maps, and anatomical diagrams, so educators produce raised tactile graphics using microcapsule swell paper or precision graphic embossers. Swell paper coats thick cotton stock with thermally reactive polymer microcapsules; after printing bold black ink lines from a standard laser printer or marker, passing the sheet under an infrared heater causes black regions to absorb energy and puff cleanly upward.

Graphic embossers instead strike paper directly with solenoid-driven hammers to create dense dot grids at varying elevations. Effective tactile graphics simplify dense visual clutter, maintain generous tactile clearance between adjacent lines and textures, and pair distinct fill patterns with Grade 1 or Grade 2 braille callout labels so readers explore spatial relationships systematically by hand.
