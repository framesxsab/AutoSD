# William Moon and Simplified Line Type

In 1845, English educator William Moon designed an embossed reading alphabet specifically aimed at adults who lost their sight later in life and found six-dot braille cells difficult to master with calloused fingertips. Moon type simplified familiar Latin capital letters into bold, open geometric strokes made of straight lines, wide angles, and partial arcs that retained recognizable shapes.

To minimize finger fatigue and help beginners track from one line to the next without losing their place, Moon printers laid out successive lines in alternating left-to-right and right-to-left boustrophedon order. Raised curved bracket guides linked the end of one line directly into the start of the following line. Although six-dot braille eventually dominated formal schooling and digital displays, Moon type remained valued for tactile children's books and rehabilitation programs serving older readers.
