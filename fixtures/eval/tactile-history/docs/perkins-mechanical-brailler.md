# The Perkins Mechanical Brailler

Introduced in 1951 by engineer David Abraham in collaboration with Perkins School for the Blind, the Perkins Brailler transformed manual braille typing into a quiet, reliable desk workflow. Earlier slate-and-stylus punching required writing every letter backward from right to left so dots appeared in reading order when the sheet was flipped over.

The Perkins Brailler features six horizontal chord keys corresponding directly to dots one through six, flanking a central spacebar, alongside dedicated backspace, line-feed, and carriage-return keys. Pressing a chord simultaneously engages steel levers and cam springs that emboss uniform dots onto heavy paper from underneath without punching holes. Its rugged die-cast aluminum chassis and purely mechanical linkages made the machine famous for lasting decades in classrooms worldwide, enabling students to write and immediately proofread pages forward without inverting characters.
