Braille is a tactile writing system used by blind and low-vision readers. Each braille character, called a cell, is built from six dot positions arranged in a two-column by three-row matrix. Combinations of raised dots encode letters, digits, punctuation, and contractions. Grade 1 braille spells every word letter by letter, while Grade 2 braille uses contractions to shorten common words and improve reading speed. Learning braille starts with the alphabet cells, then moves to numbers and punctuation marks. Consistent dot spacing and cell height are essential so fingers can travel across a line of text without losing position.
